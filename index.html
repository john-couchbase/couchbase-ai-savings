Token savings estimator FAQ

How the estimator works and where the numbers come from.

Which Couchbase capabilities does this cover?

Serverless models in Capella Model Service, billed by the token, plus four token levers from the Couchbase AI Data Plane™: exact and semantic caching in Model Service, Agent Memory, Hybrid Search with the Vectorization Service, and Agent Catalog tool search. Each lever links to its documentation.

Where do the reduction rates come from?

Caching ranges come from the traffic shape you pick and match the Couchbase Database TCO Calculator. The Agent Memory range is Couchbase's result on the standard LoCoMo benchmark. Retrieval and tool search use figures from published research on the same techniques, so they are not Couchbase benchmarks. Every source is listed in the methodology notes.

Why is the total smaller than the levers added up?

The levers overlap. One request can miss the cache, carry agent memory, retrieve documents, and call tools all at once. The estimator combines the levers so each one only acts on the spend the others leave behind. The same tokens are never counted twice, and the total can never exceed your bill.

Why compare serverless with dedicated GPUs?

A GPU fleet sized for your peak costs the same every hour, busy or idle. Serverless models in Capella Model Service bill per token through Amazon Bedrock, with nothing to pay while your deployment is idle. Token savings also depend on it: on a fixed fleet, sending fewer tokens frees capacity but doesn't change the invoice unless you shrink the fleet.

When do dedicated GPUs cost less?

When traffic is steady and high enough to keep the fleet busy most hours. The estimator shows that case plainly instead of hiding it. Capella Model Service offers both options: serverless models billed by the token, and provisioned GPUs billed by the node-hour.

Does this include the cost of running Couchbase?

No. It estimates the model spend you could avoid. It does not net out Capella, embedding model, or Search Service costs, and semantic caching uses some compute of its own. It also leaves out the engineering time to run a GPU fleet. For infrastructure and staffing costs, use the Couchbase Database TCO Calculator.

Does caching work with serverless models?

Yes. Exact and semantic caching are available when you deploy serverless models in Capella Model Service. The cache is stored in a Capella operational cluster, and semantic caching needs an embedding model deployed alongside it.

Can I use my own numbers?

Yes. Every input is editable, including GPU rates and model prices. Open "Adjust reduction rates" to replace any lever default with a rate you've measured. The estimate is only as good as those inputs.

Do I need to share my email?

No. The estimate runs in your browser and nothing you enter is sent anywhere.

Methodology & validation notes

Two starting points

If you pay per token, the estimator starts from your current monthly token spend. If you run dedicated GPUs, it first prices the same traffic on a serverless model, then applies the levers to that serverless bill. Token savings only lower a bill that is charged per token.

Serverless vs. dedicated GPUs

dedicated GPUs per year = instances × hourly rate × hours per month × 12
serverless per year = requests per month × 12 × (input tokens × input price + output tokens × output price) ÷ 1,000,000
total savings = (dedicated − serverless) + lever savings on the serverless bill

The default fleet is two g6e.12xlarge instances (4× NVIDIA L40S each) at the 1-year reserved rate for US East, running all month. The default traffic is a placeholder for you to replace. The break-even figure shows the monthly request volume at which serverless and the fleet cost the same, using only your inputs. Model prices are Amazon Bedrock on-demand list prices for US regions, checked . Capella Model Service bills serverless models in Capella Credits, and credit consumption varies with then-current Amazon Bedrock rates, so check the rates shown when you deploy before relying on a figure. The comparison assumes the serverless model you pick meets your quality bar and that your fleet can serve the traffic you enter. It leaves out reserved-instance discounts unless you enter them in the hourly rate, and it leaves out the engineering time to run a fleet, which would widen the gap. When the fleet costs less, the estimator says so.

Lever math

Each lever you turn on removes part of your annual token spend: annual spend × share of spend × reduction rate. For caching, the share is the cache-eligible slice of traffic and the rate is the hit rate. Each lever card shows that lever on its own. The total combines them instead of adding them up: annual spend × [1 − (1 − lever 1) × (1 − lever 2) × …], where each lever term is its share × rate. In practice, each lever only acts on the spend the others leave behind, so tokens touched by more than one lever count once and the total can never exceed your bill. Low and high ends are calculated separately and shown as a range.

Exact and semantic caching (Capella Model Service)

A cache hit is a model call you don't pay for. Capella Model Service stores prompt-response pairs in a Capella operational cluster and matches new prompts by exact text or, with semantic caching, by meaning, so a paraphrased question can still hit. Caching is available for serverless models billed by the token. How often that happens depends on two properties of your traffic: how much of it is cache-eligible, and how often the cache hits within that slice. The four traffic shapes and their ranges match the Couchbase Database TCO Calculator.

    Agent Memory

    On the standard LoCoMo benchmark, Agent Memory cuts token usage by 33-45% compared to leading agent memory alternatives. The estimator applies that range to the share of spend you assign to multi-turn agent conversations. Your result depends on how your agents handle context today and on what they write to memory. Benchmark result as of .

    Fewer tokens only help if answers stay accurate, so accuracy is worth checking too. On the same benchmark, Agent Memory scores 82.3% (LLM score). The Couchbase engineering team published its test setup and results, including the design trade-offs it made for accuracy, in What We Learned Evaluating Agent Memory.

    Hybrid Search retrieval

    Published research (Li et al., EMNLP 2024) compared retrieval with long-context prompting across GPT-4o, GPT-3.5-Turbo, and Gemini-1.5-Pro. A retrieval-first approach used 38% to 61% of the long-context token count with comparable answer quality, a 39-62% reduction. Pure retrieval used even fewer tokens but gave up some accuracy, so the estimator uses the more conservative figure. Couchbase Hybrid Search and the Vectorization Service provide the retrieval layer. The figure is the paper's, not a Couchbase benchmark.

    Agent Catalog tool search

    RAG-MCP (2025) tested the pattern of retrieving only the relevant tools before the model sees the prompt. On an MCP stress test, average prompt tokens fell from 2,134 to 1,084 per query, about 49%, and tool-selection accuracy rose from 13.6% to 43.1%. Agent Catalog provides the same step through semantic search over your published tools and prompts. This is one study on one model, so treat it as a starting point and replace it with your own measurement when you have one.

    Ranges, not points

    Most levers show a low–high range because the source rates are ranges and because your spend has its own variance. Tighten the range by setting share of spend and reduction rates to what you know about your workload.

    What this tool is not

    For per-token spend, it is a top-down estimate against your current bill, not a bottom-up token model. For a GPU fleet there is no token bill to start from, so it prices your traffic bottom-up from the requests and token counts you enter. Either way it is meant to start a discovery conversation, not replace one. It does not include the cost of Couchbase products, other infrastructure, or staff time.
